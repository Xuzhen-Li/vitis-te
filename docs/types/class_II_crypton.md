# Crypton

Wicker et al. 2007 (abstract only, [doi:10.1038/nrg2165](https://doi.org/10.1038/nrg2165); body and Fig. 1 [待确认]) are the source previously used to place Crypton as the **second order in Class II subclass 1**, beside the TIR order. Fig. 1 values reported earlier, still [待确认], are tyrosine recombinase (YR), TSD 0, and occurrence F (fungi). A body sentence previously quoted, still [待确认], is: “We include the poorly known Crypton69 TEs… as a second order in subclass 1.” Subject to that unverified body sentence, Crypton is not “treated separately” from the hierarchy.

Kojima and Jurka (2011, abstract, [doi:10.1186/1759-8753-2-12](https://doi.org/10.1186/1759-8753-2-12)) later reported new Crypton families “from animals, fungi, oomycetes and diatom,” after Cryptons were “originally identified in fungi and later in the sea anemone, sea urchin and insects.” (A different DOI, 10.1186/1759-8753-2-9, was checked and is a Herves transposase paper, not this one; it is not used.)

Terminal structure is **[待确认]** because two opened sources disagree in wording:

- Goodwin, Butler and Poulter (2003, abstract, [doi:10.1099/mic.0.26529-0](https://doi.org/10.1099/mic.0.26529-0), official title *Cryptons: a group of tyrosine-recombinase-encoding DNA transposons from pathogenic fungi*): “The element contains no long repeat sequences, but is bordered by short direct repeats which may have been produced by its insertion into the host genome by recombination.” (DOI 10.1093/nar/gkg181 was checked and is a Mycoplasma paper, not this one; it is not used.)
- Wells and Feschotte (2020, full text, [doi:10.1146/annurev-genet-040620-022145](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-genet-040620-022145)) group Crypton with elements that have short TIRs.

This chapter quotes both and does not pick one termini formula.

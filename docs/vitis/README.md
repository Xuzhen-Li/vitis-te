## 6. TEs in grapevine genomes

<!-- PROSE:SECTION:T00E -->
**The first genome papers.** Jaillon et al. (2007, [doi:10.1038/nature06148](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature06148)) described the assembly as obtained from "a highly homozygous genotype" (Jaillon 2007 abstract). The Jaillon abstract does not name PN40024 or Pinot, and a full text of Jaillon 2007 was not obtained on 2026-11-15. Liu et al. (2024, [doi:10.1038/s41588-024-01967-5](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41588-024-01967-5), PMC11631756, full text) later wrote: "Although the commonly used reference genome from PN40024 was highly homozygous after nine generations of selfing, it is missing >10% of genes compared with heterozygous cultivars." The name PN40024 and the nine generations of selfing are cited from that Liu sentence, not from the Jaillon abstract. Jaillon et al. 2007 is the cited source of the sentence "41.4% (average value) of the grapevine genome is composed of repetitive/transposable elements." That sentence is **not** in the opened Jaillon abstract, so the 41.4% figure remains **[待确认]**. If confirmed, it is a combined repetitive and transposable-element fraction rather than a TE-only proportion or a current annotation (type teaching is in [../types/README.md](../types/README.md)). Velasco et al. (2007, [doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0001326](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0001326)) assembled a heterozygous Pinot Noir clone, estimated the genome size at 504.6 Mb, and reported that "Homologous chromosomes of Pinot Noir differ by 11.2% of their DNA (hemizygous DNA plus chromosomal gaps)". A TE fraction was not taken from the Velasco abstract, as it does not state one.

**Heterozygosity and haplotypes.** Because the two haplotypes of a grapevine can differ substantially, a TE may be present on one haplotype and absent on the other. A collapsed haploid representation can hide that presence or absence, and a library built from only one haplotype will miss some families. This is one reason later work moved to haplotype-resolved assemblies and pangenome methods (see also the field-difficulty note in [../detection/tools_and_pipelines.md](../detection/tools_and_pipelines.md)).

**Pangenomes and presence/absence.** Liu et al. (2024, [doi:10.1038/s41588-024-01967-5](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41588-024-01967-5)) built a graph-based grapevine pangenome (Grapepan v.1.0) from 18 new phased telomere-to-telomere assemblies and 11 published assemblies, along with a variation map containing 9,105,787 short variants and 236,449 structural variants from 466 cultivars. They reported that, on average, 88.6% of the SVs overlapped TEs (Liu 2024 full text: "On average, 88.6% of the SVs overlapped TEs, whereas 30.9% intersected gene structures (Supplementary Fig. 7c,d )."; overlap with TEs, not a count of SVs that are TE insertions). For TE presence/absence across a panel, this repository points to panEDTA (Ou et al. 2024, [doi:10.1101/gr.278131.123](https://doi.org/10.1101/gr.278131.123)), rather than a simple concatenation of raw EDTA libraries (see the practical route in [../detection/tools_and_pipelines.md](../detection/tools_and_pipelines.md)).
